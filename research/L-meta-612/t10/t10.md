# L-meta-612, route T10: attacking DV2-Phi on girth >= 5 (target L-meta-608)

Status: **NOT closed.** girth >= 5 remains open. New in this round:

* **PROVED, Lemma P (second-use lemma).** This needs only C4-freeness. If a node y has y(e_c) = 0 and carries two
  neighbours w1, w2 of c as *singletons*, then the first AND gate q on any path above y does one of four things.
  It violates N1, N2 or N3, or it is a **re-creation gate** q = r ∧ s with sing(s) = {c}, which accepts cw1 and cw2
  purely. In that last case q is itself a Fact-C creator of both edges.
* **PROVED, Corollary P2.** A singleton-type pen witness at c (DV2) forces, on every live path above it, an
  N1/N2/N3 violation or a live second creator of the edges cw_i. DATA: 7,322 singleton-type witnesses, 0 exceptions.
* **DATA: Lemma P does not cover the real pen.** 3,413 of 10,735 pen witnesses on girth-5 circuits are
  *pure* or *mixed*: y accepts c-w purely, so the edge c-w was already created below y. For these the first
  live AND is unconstrained (3,376 "other" cases).
* **REFUTED: pen-free DV2 on girth >= 5**, i.e. LD_A(c) >= k_Z + k_L + |N_D(c)|. Smallest counterexample: P4 at
  Phi+1, `4=1|3 5=0&4 6=2&4 7=1&6 8=5|7`, c = 0. There are 8,105 violations in the corpus. The pen term is genuine.
* **PROVED, Proposition F (2-slice subadditivity).** Let w(p) = |E_p| + |S_p| − 1. OR gates raise w by at most 1, and AND gates
  whose rectangle edge set is a forest do not raise it. Consequences: every *formula* for f_G whose rectangles
  are forests has #OR >= |E| − 1, and every clean formula has size >= Phi. What fails for circuits is exactly
  fan-out.
* **REFUTED (construction), per-node OR potentials.** "#OR(cone p) >= |E_p| + |S_p| − 1 for every node" holds
  at every node of all 88,987 circuits in the corpus. It fails on the Heawood graph (girth 6): the single node
  OR(points) ∧ OR(lines) has 12 ORs and E_p = E, which needs 20. So no node-by-node induction can prove (OR) on
  girth >= 5. Junk rectangles on graphs with 6-cycles cover more edges than their ORs pay for.
* **REFUTED: T2-LD on girth >= 5.** T2-LD says some A/N/B option is paid by constants, copies and dead gates alone.
  The new fuzz campaign finds a counterexample on **Petersen, size 24 = Phi+4**, which is N3-only (junk) (§6), with
  max(LD − need) = −1. It holds on all 38,971 girth-5 circuits of the pools and cache, so only adversarial search finds the failure.
  **T2-LR survives**: N(5) is paid through the Top merge (LR = 12 = need). So the Top merge is needed on girth >= 5 too,
  and the right non-vacuous target is **T2-LR(g5)**.
* **DATA.** DV2
  (in its full form, pen <= k_Z − δ_c) certifies every girth-5 circuit of size Phi (7,053). Uncertified circuits
  first appear at Phi+1: 24 at Phi+1, 86 at Phi+2, 80 at Phi+3.
* **REFUTED on girth >= 5: five localisations of the good option** (§4). **Local exchange to STAR-ALL is stuck
  at Phi+1.** Single-gate edits leave 45 circuits stuck. Adding the distributive move still leaves 25 stuck; the smallest
  is P4 at Phi+1 (§3).

Labels: PROVED (full proof here), DATA (machine check, scripts in this folder), REFUTED (explicit circuit
checked on all 2^n inputs, or an explicit construction that was checked). The notation is that of t2/t7/t8: S_p = sing(p), and p *accepts e = uv
purely* if p(e_u + e_v) = 1 and p(e_u) = p(e_v) = 0. E_p is the set of edges of G accepted purely by p. For an AND gate h = a ∧ b,
K = S_a ∩ S_b, A = S_a \ K, B = S_b \ K, and R_h = A x B.

## 0. Why DV2-Phi, as stated, is not a usable target (and what is)

DV2-Phi says: "no DV2-certified vertex implies size >= Phi + 1". On girth-5 graphs it is consistent with all data. Every
size-Phi circuit is certified, and uncertified ones exist from Phi+1 on (C5 with three pendants at Phi+1; the
t8 fuzzer: C5{012} at Phi+1, and minpaid = 1 on sun5, P6, n8 and psun at Phi+1). It is a *lower bound statement*
on a subclass, though. Proving it needs a size lower-bound technique for uncertified circuits, and these are
exactly the circuits with junk or singleton-carrying ANDs that Theorems S/S2 do not cover. So DV2-Phi is at least as
hard as the claim restricted to non-clean circuits. It cannot be obtained by a local argument at one vertex (§3).

The natural non-vacuous target was T2-LD(g5): for every girth >= 5 non-star G and every reduced C for f_G, some option
ρ ∈ {A(v), N(v), B(v)} has LD(ρ) = |Z ∪ L| >= need(ρ). It holds on all 38,971 corpus circuits, but it is **REFUTED by
fuzzing** (Petersen, Phi+4, §6). What survives is

**T2-LR(g5).** The same with LR (rule R plus the Top merge, t7) in place of LD.

Every LR gate is eliminated, so T2-LR(g5) + R-TOT-F (girth >= 5 is closed under G − v and G − N[v]) gives
L-meta-608. It is a statement about *all* reduced circuits, so the min-cex caveat does not apply. DATA: no failure on
the pools (t7 an5) or on t7's LR fuzzing (size <= Phi+3). The Petersen T2-LD counterexample is LR-paid only at N(5). Note: the fuzzer that found it
optimises LD, not LR, so T2-LR has had no new adversarial test at Phi+4.

## 1. Lemma P (second-use lemma). PROVED, any C4-free G, any monotone circuit

**Lemma P.** Let c be a vertex and y a node with y(e_c) = 0, and let W = S_y ∩ N(c) with |W| >= 2. Let q = r ∧ s be an
AND gate with r >= y (for example, r is reached from y through OR gates only). Then at least one of the following holds:
* (N1) S_r ∩ S_s ≠ ∅;
* (N3) q accepts a non-edge purely;
* (N2) S_s = ∅;
* (REC) S_s = {c}, c ∉ S_r, and q accepts cw purely for every w ∈ W, while neither r nor s accepts cw purely.
  So q is a Fact-C creator of every edge cw, w ∈ W.

*Proof.* Assume that (N1), (N2) and (N3) fail, and take u ∈ S_s. Since r >= y, W ⊆ S_r. By the failure of (N1), u ∉ S_r and
W ∩ S_s = ∅. For w ∈ W: q(e_w + e_u) = r(e_w + e_u) s(e_w + e_u) = 1, q(e_w) = s(e_w) = 0, and q(e_u) = r(e_u) = 0.
So q accepts wu purely, and since (N3) fails, wu ∈ E. Hence u is a common neighbour of two distinct vertices w1, w2 ∈ W,
and u ∉ W because u ∉ S_r. The vertex c is also a common neighbour, so C4-freeness gives u = c. Therefore S_s = {c}.
Then c ∉ S_r, so q(e_c) = r(e_c) = 0, q(e_w) = 0 and q(e_c + e_w) = 1. Also r(e_w) = 1 and s(e_c) = 1, so neither input
accepts cw purely. ∎

**Corollary P2 (PROVED).** Let ρ = (x_c := 0), and let y ∈ I_c be a pen witness of *singleton type*, meaning W ⊆ S_y with
|W| >= 2 (t8 §2). Every path from y to the output that avoids K_c contains an AND gate. On each such path the first
AND gate q satisfies (N1), (N2), (N3) or (REC). In case (REC), the input s ∉ K_c ∪ {x_c} has s >= x_c and s ≠ x_c. So
the live gate q creates the edges cw (w ∈ W) a second time, besides the dead-zone creation that put w into N_D(c).

*Proof.* Such a path exists because y ∉ Z_c. ORs only enlarge S, while the output has S = ∅ and |S_y| >= 2, so the path
contains an AND gate. The input r of the first AND on the path is >= y. If s were in K_c, or s = x_c, then q would be in
K_c (t8 Lemma DG (i)), which is impossible since q lies on the path. s(e_c) = 1 gives s >= x_c. ∎

DATA (`p4.py`, girth-5 corpus, all A options): 10,735 witnesses y ∈ I_c with |S_c(y) ∩ N(c)| >= 2.
| witness type | count | first live AND: N1 / N2 / N3 / REC / other |
|---|---|---|
| singleton (W ⊆ S_y) | 7,322 | 134 / 245 / 6,918 / 31 / **0** |
| mixed | 3,332 | 1 / 6 / 28 / 0 / 3,313 |
| pure (W ∩ S_y = ∅) | 81 | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 63 |

(Counts are per witness; a witness can have several live paths.) So Lemma P is sharp on its own hypothesis, but a third of
the penalty comes from y that *already contains* a c-star (y accepts cw purely, y(e_c) = 0, y ∉ D_c). These are
gates such as y = (x_c ∧ x_w2) ∨ x_w1 ∨ ..., which are shared between the dead zone of c and a live use. Lemma P says
nothing about them, and they are the main obstruction to DV2-Phi.

**Pen-free DV2 is REFUTED** (`p1.py`). LD_A(c) >= k_Z + k_L + |N_D(c)| fails in 8,105 of 264,712 A-options on girth-5
circuits. Smallest: P4 [01,03,12], size 5 = Phi+1, `4=1|3 5=0&4 6=2&4 7=1&6 8=5|7`, c = 0. Gate 4 = x1|x3 carries
{1,3} = N(0) into Z_0 and is reused by the junk gate 6 = x2 & (x1|x3) (junk 23, killed by 7). LD = 2 < 3. Here
Lemma P's case (N3) occurs, but the junk costs a gate *outside* Z_0, so A(0) does not see it.

## 2. Proposition F (2-slice subadditivity). PROVED, any C4-free G

For a node p let E_p = {e ∈ E : p accepts e purely}, and w(p) = |E_p| + |S_p| − 1 if E_p ∪ S_p ≠ ∅, w(p) = 0 otherwise.
Inputs have w = 0, and the output has w = |E| − 1.

**Proposition F.**
(i) For p = a ∨ b: w(p) <= w(a) + w(b) + 1.
(ii) For h = a ∧ b whose rectangle edge set R_h ∩ E is a forest: w(h) <= w(a) + w(b). The rectangle edge set is a forest
whenever h is clean at h (then R_h is a star), and whenever min(|A|, |B|) <= 2 (C4-freeness).
(iii) Hence every monotone **formula** (fan-out 1) for f_G in which every rectangle edge set is a forest has
#OR >= |E| − 1. Every **clean** formula has size >= |E| + tau − 1 = Phi.

*Proof.* (i) S_p = S_a ∪ S_b. If e = uv ∈ E_p, then u, v ∉ S_a ∪ S_b, and a or b accepts e, necessarily purely. So
E_p ⊆ E_a ∪ E_b. If a and b are both nonempty-type, the bound follows by adding. If a is empty-type, then
w(p) <= w(b) <= w(b) + 1.
(ii) S_h = K. *Claim: E_h ⊆ E_a ∪ E_b ∪ (R_h ∩ E).* Take e = uv ∈ E_h, so u, v ∉ K. If neither a nor b accepts e purely, then
a accepts it through a singleton, say u ∈ S_a, so u ∈ A. Also b accepts it through a singleton. That singleton is not u
(u ∈ S_b would put u in K), so it is v, and v ∈ B. Thus e ∈ R_h.
* If S_a = ∅ (or S_b = ∅), then A = ∅, and every e ∈ E_h is accepted purely by a. If moreover E_a = ∅, then w(h) = 0.
* If S_a = S_b = ∅, then E_h ⊆ E_a ∩ E_b and w(h) <= w(a).
* Otherwise both inputs are nonempty-type. With κ = |K|, α = |A|, β = |B|, we need
  |R_h ∩ E| + κ − 1 <= 2κ + α + β − 2. A forest on the α + β vertices of A ∪ B has at most α + β − 1 edges, so this holds.
  It also holds if R_h ∩ E = ∅, because then S_a ∪ S_b ≠ ∅.
* If min(|A|, |B|) <= 2, a cycle in the bipartite graph R_h ∩ E would contain two vertices of the small side with two
  common neighbours, which is a C4.

(iii) Unfold the formula into its tree. Summing (i) and (ii) from the leaves up gives
|E| − 1 = w(out) <= #OR + Σ_leaves w(x_u) = #OR. If the formula is clean, Fact C applied to each edge gives a star
creator whose centre lies in the edge. So the centres of the AND gates cover E, and #AND >= tau (t2 Lemma A). ∎

*Where it fails for circuits.* For a DAG, (i) and (ii) give only Σ_{OR t} π(t) >= |E| − 1, where π(t) is the number of
paths from t to the output. Fan-out is the entire obstruction. Theorem S handles it under STAR-ALL by the C4
disjointness of leaf cones, and no extension is known.

DATA (`p8.py`): (i) holds at all 237,659 OR gates of the girth-5 corpus, and (ii) at all 149,931 AND gates. Every
rectangle edge set in the corpus is a forest, including the Petersen circuits.

**Per-node potentials are REFUTED (construction, `heawood.py`).** On all 88,987 C4-free circuits of the corpus,
#OR(cone p) >= w(p) holds at **every** node (`p6.py`, `p7.py`, W2). On clean circuits even
|cone p| >= |E_p| + tau(E_p) + |S_p| − 1 holds at every node. But on the Heawood graph H (girth 6, 14 vertices,
21 edges, Phi = 27), the node p = OR(points) ∧ OR(lines) has 12 ORs in its cone and E_p = E(H), S_p = ∅, while w(p) = 20.
Because that node's cone is itself a circuit, **no proof of (OR) on girth >= 5 can go node by node**. A junk
rectangle on a graph with 6-cycles creates up to |A| |B|^{1/2} edges for |A| + |B| − 2 ORs, and only the later
junk killing can pay. This is the Jukna-type phenomenon, and it is already present at girth 6. The small-graph data
could not see it: no rectangle in the corpus has a non-forest edge set.

## 3. What a local proof of DV2-Phi would have to beat (REFUTED / DATA)

All on the girth-5 corpus (`p3.py`; pools + t2 cache, 38,971 circuits, Phi..Phi+4). paid = LD-paid.
| statement | status, smallest failure |
|---|---|
| T2-LD (some A/N/B paid) | 0 failures on the corpus. REFUTED by fuzzing (Petersen, Phi+4, §6) |
| some A or B option paid | REFUTED: 4 circuits, Petersen at Phi+2 |
| V1: every minimal AND has a support vertex with a paid option | REFUTED: 289 circuits; Pet-v Phi+2 |
| V2: some minimal AND has a support vertex with A or B paid | REFUTED: 20; [01,04,05,12,16,23,34] at Phi+2 |
| V4: some minimal star x_c & OR_B has A(c) paid or a paid option at some b ∈ B | REFUTED: 4; Petersen Phi+2 |
| V5: every minimal star: A(c) or an option at B ∪ {c} paid | REFUTED: 271; Pet-v Phi+2 |
| every vertex within distance 1 of a paid vertex | REFUTED: 2,005 circuits have a vertex at distance 2 from every paid vertex, and 16 at distance 3 (Phi+1..Phi+3) |
The full circuits are in `p3.log`. So a proof cannot place the paid option next to a pen witness. A pen at c
is compensated, when it is compensated, at distance 2 or 3.

**Exchange (`ex1.py`).** Measure (size, #violating ANDs), lexicographic. A single-gate edit followed by reduction
improves the measure for every non-STAR-ALL girth-5 circuit tested at size Phi (15) and at Phi+3/Phi+4 (46). It is
**stuck** for 43 of 180 at Phi+1 and 2 of 150 at Phi+2. Smallest: C5, size 8,
`5=0|3 6=0|4 7=1|3 8=1|4 9=2&7 10=6&8 11=5&10 12=9|11`. Gate 10 = (x0|x4)&(x1|x4) = x4 ∨ x0x1 carries the
singleton 4 (N1). The improving move is the two-gate distributive rewrite (a|x)&(b|x) → x|(a&b), which saves a gate.
A normalisation proof by local moves therefore needs at least the distributive move. Adding that move to greedy descent
(`ex2.py`) takes 20 of the 45 stuck circuits to a STAR-ALL circuit of size Phi. **25 stay stuck** at Phi+1/Phi+2 with violations.
The smallest is P4 at Phi+1, `4=0|1 5=0|2 6=1|3 7=5&6 8=4&7`: the junk rectangle 7 plus the killer 8. Removing it needs a
rewrite of three gates at once, the "template" rewrite of t2 §3. So local-move normalisation is REFUTED for
these move sets, already on a tree.

**Why the uncertified circuits fail (`p8.py`).** There are 190 uncertified girth-5 circuits (Phi+1..Phi+3). In every one, the
vertex-level failure reasons are mixed: some vertex fails the carrier hypothesis (189 circuits), some has k_Z < δ_c (181), and some has
pen > 0 (151). **No uncertified circuit fails by pen alone.** So DV2-Phi also needs the carrier/singleton defects and the
"all readers of x_c are live ORs" defect to be charged, not only pen. Their STAR-ALL violation profiles: N2+N3 148, N1 27,
N3 9, N1+N2+N3 3, none 3. The last 3 are STAR-ALL, so Theorem S covers them.

## 4. Exact gap for girth >= 5

PROVED so far: Theorem S (STAR-ALL), Theorem S2 (N1 + N3), Lemma A (clean ⇒ #AND >= tau), Corollary M,
Leaf/Cycle Lemmas, R-TOT-F, Lemma DG, Theorem DV2, Corollary SL, Lemma P / P2 and Proposition F. A minimal girth-5 counterexample
C (size Phi − 1) therefore
1. violates N1 or N3 (Theorem S2);
2. has, at every vertex c, pen(c) > k_Z(c) − δ_c or a failed carrier hypothesis (DV2);
3. is not a formula with forest rectangles (Proposition F), and is not of AND-depth 1 (Corollary SL);
4. satisfies the structure of Corollary M at leaves.

The missing step can be stated in two equivalent-strength forms:
* (LR form) T2-LR(g5), §0. The LD form is refuted, so a proof must count output absorption (Top merge);
* (count form) **(OR-g5)** #OR >= |E| − 1 together with **(AND-g5)** #AND >= tau for every circuit. Here (AND) is open only for
  junk circuits and (OR) only through fan-out (Proposition F). The Heawood construction shows that (OR) needs a
  global junk-killing argument.

Both require charging a *mixed or pure pen witness* (a gate that already contains a c-star and is used both dead and live
under x_c := 0) or a *junk rectangle* to a gate outside the restricted zone. No such charging is known. Lemma P
handles only the singleton witnesses, and turns them into violations or double creations.

DATA on clean circuits (`p5.py`, 73,297 clean C4-free circuits): (OR), (AND) and size >= Phi hold everywhere. The nf.md split
(B) B2 >= |E| − m0c fails even on clean circuits: 2 girth-5 N1 circuits at Phi+2, for example on C5 with pendants at 0, 1 and 2,
`8=2&7 9=4|1 10=9|5 11=10&0 12=3|1 13=9&12 14=3&4 15=8|14 16=13&6 17=16|2 18=17&12 19=15|11 20=19|18`.

## 5. Lean outline (for the PROVED parts only)

* `Circuit`, `eval`, `sing p := {u | eval p (e u)}`, `acceptsPure p u v` (existing L_meta_599 infrastructure).
* Lemma P: `lemmaP (hC4 : C4Free G) (hy : eval y (e c) = false) (hW : w1 ≠ w2 ∧ w1 ∈ sing y ∩ N c ∧ w2 ∈ sing y ∩ N c)
  (hr : ∀ x, eval y x → eval r x) : N1 q ∨ N3 q ∨ N2 q ∨ REC q c`. The proof is pure evaluation on e_w, e_u and e_w + e_u,
  plus the C4 lemma `common_nbr_unique : C4Free G → w1 ≠ w2 → u ∈ N w1 ∩ N w2 → c ∈ N w1 ∩ N w2 → u = c`.
* Proposition F: define `w p := (E_p).card + (sing p).card - 1` over `Finset`; prove `E_or_subset`, `E_and_subset`,
  then `forest_card_le` (a forest on k vertices has at most k − 1 edges; Mathlib `SimpleGraph.IsAcyclic.card_edgeFinset`), then induction on a
  formula (tree) datatype.
